Most "my opener is dead" calls we take in {{city}} turn out to be something simple: a lock button pressed by accident, an unplugged outlet, a sensor that got bumped. Work through these seven checks in order before you book a service call. Each takes a minute or two, and if the door still won't move at the end, you'll know exactly what to tell us.

1. Try the wall button, not just the remote

Press the button on the wall inside the garage. If the door runs from the wall but not from the remote, the opener is fine and the problem is the remote: a dead battery, lost programming, or a car parked out of range. Replace the coin cell or 9-volt battery first; it is the single most common fix we see. If that doesn't help, press the "learn" button on the motor unit, then the remote button within thirty seconds to re-pair it.

If neither the wall button nor the remote does anything, keep going. The problem is at the opener.

2. Check for lock mode

Most multi-button wall consoles have a lock or "vacation" switch that disables the remotes while keeping the wall button active. The giveaway: the wall button works but every remote and the keypad are ignored, and the console light often blinks. Press and hold the lock button for a few seconds until the blinking stops, then try the remote again.

3. Look at the photo eyes

The two small sensors a few inches off the floor on each side of the opening must see each other for the door to close. If the door opens normally but won't close, or closes a few inches and reverses while the opener light flashes, the photo eyes are the suspect. Both should show a steady LED. A blinking or dark LED means the pair is out of alignment, a lens is dirty, or a wire has pulled loose. Wipe the lenses, gently bend the bracket until both lights go solid, and move anything (a trash can, a bike tire, leaves) breaking the beam. Low afternoon sun on one lens can also cause intermittent trouble.

4. Confirm the opener has power

If the opener light is off and nothing hums when you press the button, check the outlet. The ceiling receptacle is easy to knock loose when storing things in the rafters, and it is often on a GFCI circuit shared with the garage wall outlets. Press the reset, check the breaker, and plug a lamp into the outlet to confirm it is live. If the outlet is good and the opener is still dark, the logic board or transformer has likely failed, a repair we can usually do on the first visit.

5. Listen for the motor running without the door moving

This is the one that fools people. You press the button, the opener hums for a few seconds, and the door stays put. On chain and belt openers that usually means the nylon main drive gear has stripped its teeth. Look inside the housing for white shavings. It is a common failure on openers past ten years, and a gear-and-sprocket kit costs far less than a new unit. Our opener repair technicians in {{city}} carry gear kits for the major brands.

If the motor runs and the trolley moves along the rail but the door doesn't follow, the trolley has simply been disconnected. Pull the red release cord toward the motor until it clicks and re-engages, then run the opener once.

6. Check the travel and force limits

Two adjustments on the motor unit tell the opener how far to run and how hard to push. If the door stops short of the floor, stops a few inches from fully open, or reverses just before it finishes closing with nothing in the way, a limit has drifted. The screws or buttons are labeled "up" and "down" on the unit; small quarter-turn adjustments are all it takes. One caution: if the door reverses because it is heavy or binding, turning up the force hides a real problem and defeats the safety reversal.

7. Lift the door by hand

Disconnect the opener with the release cord and lift the door yourself. A balanced door lifts with one hand and stays put at waist height. If it feels like dead weight, drifts down on its own, or lifts crooked, the opener was never the problem. A broken or weak spring, a frayed cable, or a roller off its track is what stopped the door, and it is why a new opener on an unbalanced door burns out in a year or two. Don't force the door up; the parts are under heavy tension, and spring and cable repair is a same-day job for us.
